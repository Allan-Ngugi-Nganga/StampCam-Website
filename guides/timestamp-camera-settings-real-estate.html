Timestamp camera settings for real estate

Updated 5 October 2026 · 5 min read

Real estate runs on two kinds of photos: the ones that sell a property, and the ones that record its condition. They need different settings, and mixing them up causes problems.

The two jobs

Marketing photos should be clean and uncluttered, with no overlay if the listing platform does not want one. Condition photos are the opposite: the more visible information, the better, because they exist to be evidence.

For condition and move-in, move-out records

  • Use a stamp with date, time and location on every photo.
  • Verify the clock so the record is consistent.
  • Shoot rooms in the same order every time, so two visits are comparable.
  • Capture wide shots, then close ups of any existing damage.
  • Add a one line note per shot, for example the room and the item.
  • Export a single PDF for the tenant, landlord or client file.

For listings

  • Keep the shot clean, or turn the stamp off if the platform prefers it.
  • Capture in good light, and keep verticals straight.
  • Keep a second timestamped set for your own file even if the listing photos are clean.

A simple default

Keep the date and time format consistent across your whole portfolio. A short format such as YYYY-MM-DD reads clearly and sorts correctly. Put the stamp in a corner that does not cover the subject, and keep the font large enough to read on a phone.

Why this protects you

A dated, located, consistent record is what settles a deposit dispute or a condition claim. It shows the state of the property on a specific day, without relying on memory or editable metadata.
