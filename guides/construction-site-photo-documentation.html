Construction site photo documentation that holds up

Updated 5 October 2026 · 6 min read

Good site photos do two jobs: they show progress, and they protect you when there is a dispute. The difference between a useful record and a folder of random shots is a system.

What to capture, every visit

  • Progress. Wide shots of each area, taken from the same spot each time so change is obvious.
  • Defects and snags. Close ups, plus a wider shot for context.
  • Safety and compliance. Signage, barriers, PPE and anything the inspector will ask about.
  • Deliveries and plant. Materials on arrival, their condition, and any serial numbers.
  • Existing conditions. Before you touch anything, especially near a neighbour's property.

Why timestamps matter on site

On a disputed job, the first question is when the photo was taken and where. A date, time and location burned into the image answers both without argument. It also proves you inspected on the day you said you did.

A repeatable routine

  1. Create a project per job so photos do not mix. In StampCam, hashtags such as #NorthWing group shots automatically.
  2. Shoot the same sequences each visit so reports are comparable.
  3. Add a one line note to each key shot: location and what it shows.
  4. Export a PDF at the end of the visit, while you are still on site.
  5. Send the report the same day, and keep the PDF with the project file.

What goes in the handover report

  • A cover sheet with the project, the date and who prepared it.
  • Photos in a logical order, each with its timestamp and location.
  • Notes that read as a record, not a caption.
