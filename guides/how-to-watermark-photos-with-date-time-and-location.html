How to watermark photos with date, time and location

Updated 5 October 2026 · 4 min read

A watermark is the visible layer that turns a photo into a record. Getting the content and placement right makes it readable instead of cluttered.

What to include

  • Date, in one unambiguous format.
  • Time, with the time zone when it matters.
  • Coordinates, and a human readable place if you have one.
  • Altitude and heading where orientation is useful.
  • A short note for context.

Where to place it

Pick a corner that does not cover the subject, and keep the same corner across a set. Make the font large enough to read on a phone.

Keeping a set consistent

  • One date format across every photo.
  • One font size and colour.
  • The same field order every time.

Note on editing apps

Overlaying text after the fact produces an edited image. For anything that has to hold up, stamp at capture.

Questions

  • Should the watermark be transparent? A solid or lightly translucent panel keeps text readable against any background.
  • Can I turn the watermark off? Yes. Use a clean capture for listing photos and a stamped one for documentation.
