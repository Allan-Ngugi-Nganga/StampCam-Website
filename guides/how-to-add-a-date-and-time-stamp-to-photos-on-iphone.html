How to add a date and time stamp to photos on iPhone

Updated 5 October 2026 · 5 min read

By default an iPhone photo carries its date and time in hidden metadata, not on the picture. If you need a date you can actually see, you have to burn it into the image.

What the iPhone does by default

The iPhone Camera app records the date and time in a metadata field called EXIF. You can read it in the Photos app, but nothing appears on the photo itself. That is fine for a camera roll and useless for a document.

Why hidden metadata is not enough

EXIF is stripped whenever a photo passes through many messaging apps, gets cropped, is exported for the web or is screenshotted. It can also be edited with free desktop tools. If the date needs to survive sharing, it has to be part of the image.

Two ways to get a visible stamp

  • Burn it in. An app draws the date and time into the pixels at capture, so it travels with the photo everywhere.
  • Overlay it after. Editing apps can add text, but that is slower, easy to forget, and the result is an edited image, which is weaker for anything evidentiary.

Steps in StampCam

  1. Open StampCam and choose the Standard mode.
  2. Pick your date format, for example YYYY-MM-DD.

Getting the details right

  • Turn on automatic time on the iPhone so the clock is accurate.
  • Keep one date format across a set of photos so they read as a record.
  • Place the stamp in a corner that does not cover the subject.

Questions

  • Does the iPhone camera add a timestamp? No. It stores the date in metadata but does not print it on the photo. You need an app that burns the date into the image.
  • Can I add a date stamp to photos I already took? Yes, with an editing app, but the result is an edited file. For documentation it is better to stamp at capture.
