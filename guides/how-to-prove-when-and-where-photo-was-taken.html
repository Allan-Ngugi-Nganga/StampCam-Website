How to prove when and where a photo was taken

Updated 5 October 2026 · 4 min read

Anyone can say a photo was taken on a certain date at a certain place. Proving it is a different problem. Here is what actually holds up, and where the usual methods fall down.

The two ways a photo carries a time and place

Every phone photo can carry two kinds of information. The first is metadata, the hidden EXIF fields that record the capture time and GPS position. The second is what is visible in the image itself.

Metadata is convenient but easy to lose or change. The visible record is harder to fake and impossible to strip without altering the picture.

Why EXIF alone is not enough

  • It is removed by many messaging apps, social platforms and export tools.
  • It can be edited with free desktop software.
  • It is invisible, so a reviewer has to take your word that it is genuine.
  • It does not travel with a printed or scanned copy.

What makes a record stronger

  • Visible stamp. The date, time and coordinates are drawn into the image, so they survive sharing.
  • Verified clock. The timestamp is checked against network time, so a manually changed phone clock shows up.
  • Consistency. The same format across every photo in a set, which reads as a deliberate record.
  • Context. A short note and a set of shots that show overview, detail and surroundings.

How to do it

  1. Turn on automatic time on your phone.
  2. Capture with an app that burns the stamp into the image.
  3. Enable network time verification if the app offers it.
  4. Keep the stamp format consistent across the set.
  5. Export a dated PDF so the whole set is one file.

What it does and does not guarantee

A verified, burned-in stamp is a strong documentation aid. It shows what the device and network reported at capture time. It is not a cryptographic proof and not a legal guarantee, and it does not replace the requirements of a specific insurer, court or client.
