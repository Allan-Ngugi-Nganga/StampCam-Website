How to photograph damage for an insurance claim

Updated 5 October 2026 · 5 min read

Adjusters are not judging whether damage exists. They are judging whether your photos are trustworthy and complete. A routine shot list removes the guesswork.

The shot list

  1. A wide shot that places the property or vehicle in context.
  2. A mid shot of the affected area.
  3. Close ups of each distinct piece of damage.
  4. Any serial, plate or model number.
  5. A shot of anything that changes your claim, such as a temporary repair.

Keep it trustworthy

  • Leave the phone clock on automatic and use a network time check.
  • Do not edit, crop or filter.
  • Shoot in good light, and describe anything that is hard to see.
  • Add a one line note with the claim reference.

File it as one document

Rather than sending a dozen loose images, export a single PDF with a cover sheet and the photos. It is easier for the adjuster and easier for you to reference later.

Questions

  • How many photos should I take? Enough to tell the story without gaps: usually one wide, one mid and several close ups per item of damage.
  • Should I include a photo of the date? The date should be visible on every photo, ideally verified. You should not need a separate photo of a clock.
