What makes a photo usable as evidence

Updated 5 October 2026 · 6 min read

A photo on its own proves very little. What makes it useful is a set of qualities that together show when it was taken, where, and that it has not been altered.

The five qualities

  • Time. A date and time, ideally verified against network time so a changed clock is visible.
  • Place. Coordinates, and enough context to recognise the location.
  • Authenticity. No edits after capture that could be seen as tampering.
  • Clarity. In focus, well lit, with the relevant detail visible.
  • Continuity. A coherent set, in order, with a plausible story.

Why edits hurt

Cropping, filters and retouching all change the file. Even when innocent, they invite the question of what else changed. For documentation, shoot it right the first time and leave it alone.

Making the set coherent

  • Wide shot for context, mid shot, then close up of the detail.
  • Keep one consistent stamp format across the set.
  • Add short notes so each photo stands alone.
  • Export the set as one dated document.

What it still is not

A strong documentation aid is not a cryptographic guarantee and does not replace the rules of a particular court, insurer or client. Treat it as the best available record, not an absolute.

Questions

  • Do photos need to be originals? Originals are safer. If you must edit, keep a copy of the original and be able to explain any change.
  • Is a screenshot of a photo useful? Rarely. A screenshot loses the original image data and the location.
