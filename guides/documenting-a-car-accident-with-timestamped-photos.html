Documenting a car accident with timestamped photos

Updated 5 October 2026 · 4 min read

At a collision, memory is unreliable and time is short. A fixed order of shots captures the essentials before anything changes.

At the scene, in order

  1. An overview of the whole scene, with both vehicles.
  2. Each vehicle from four corners.
  3. Number plates and any visible damage close up.
  4. Road conditions, signals and any skid marks.
  5. Anything that shows the weather and time of day.

After the event

  • Photograph your vehicle again the next day, in daylight.
  • Photograph any damage that appears later.
  • Keep every photo unedited and timestamped.

Why the timestamp matters

Disputes often turn on when damage appeared. A verifiable date on every photo shows what existed at the scene and what appeared afterwards.

Questions

  • Should I photograph the other driver? Photograph vehicles, plates and the scene. Do not photograph people without reason and permission.
  • Do I need location on the photos? Yes. Coordinates tie the photos to the exact spot, which matters at junctions and car parks.
